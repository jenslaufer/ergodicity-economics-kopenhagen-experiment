# Kopenhagen Experiment in Ergodicity Economics

## Overview

The Kopenhagen experiment explores the fundamental assumptions of ergodicity in economic systems, challenging the traditional economic models that assume ensemble averages equal time averages.

## Background

Ergodicity economics questions whether individual economic experiences mirror population-wide statistics over time. This experiment investigates real-world scenarios where non-ergodic behavior emerges in economic decision-making.

## Key Concepts

- **Ergodicity**: The property where time averages equal ensemble averages
- **Non-ergodic systems**: Economic environments where individual trajectories differ significantly from population statistics
- **Path dependence**: How historical sequences of events affect economic outcomes

## Methodology

The experiment analyzes economic decision-making under uncertainty, comparing:
- Traditional expected utility theory predictions
- Ergodicity economics framework outcomes
- Empirical data from real economic scenarios

## Applications

- Investment strategies
- Risk assessment models
- Policy impact evaluation
- Wealth distribution analysis
